Figure files generated by the analysis, copied by `lab/bin/writing_pack.py pull`. Each is a pgfplots picture,
written by `t3/paper_figures.py` from the files the macros come from; `t3/test_paper_figures.py` checks that every
plotted value equals the macro the text prints for it. Read one with `\input{../figures/<name>}` inside a `figure`
environment. Both need `\usepackage{pgfplots}` and `\usepgfplotslibrary{groupplots}` in the preamble, and the
macros loaded before them. They were compiled with `\pgfplotsset{compat=1.18}`; without a compat setting pgfplots
uses older defaults and the layout may shift.

- `lookup-ratio.tex` (the earlier campaign's lookup grid): one panel per table shape. The x axis is the table size in
  routes (log scale); the y axis is the median over the seed pairs of the matcher's lookup time divided by the
  fastest competitor's, with its interval. The solid line is 1 and the dashed line the non-inferiority margin
  (`\Margin`). Uses `\shape`. Values: `\HOneRatio*`, `\HOneLow*`, `\HOneHigh*`.
- `e2e-lead.tex` (this study's end-to-end campaign): one panel per system and table size. For each program (C++, Go,
  Rust), three marks on a log scale of ns per request: the matcher's lead over the next router (filled circle), the
  A/A floor (filled square) and the span of the medians over the separate processes of the matcher (open triangle).
  A lead above the square exceeds the A/A floor. The floor rule was chosen after the first records were seen, so
  which leads exceed it is exploratory; a caption must say so. Uses `\RThreeKCopies`. Values: `\RThree*Lead`,
  `\RThree*Floor`, `\RThree*KSpan`.

End-to-end latency at the lookup grid's sizes was not measured: this study measures throughput in a closed loop, at
two sizes. The end-to-end figure therefore shows the lead, the floor and the span, not latency.
